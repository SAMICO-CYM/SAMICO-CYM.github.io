## Remark
**(i)** $L^2$-Inner product: 

$$\langle f, g \rangle = \int_a^b f(x) g(x) \, dx$$

**(ii)** $L^2$-Norm:

$$\| f \| = \sqrt{\langle f, f \rangle} = \sqrt{\int_a^b \vert f(x) \vert^2 \, dx}$$

**(iii)** $L^2$-Metric:

$$d(f, g) = \| f- g\| = \sqrt{\int_a^b \vert f(x) - g(x) \vert^2 \, dx}$$

## Theorem 1 (Least-Square Approximation)
Let $\\{ X_n \\}$ be any orthogonal set of functions, and let $\| f \| < \infty.$ Let $N$ be a fixed positive integer. Among all possible choices of $N$ constants $c_1, c_2, \cdots, c_N$, the choice that minimizes 

$$\left \| f - \sum_{n=1}^N c_nX_n \right \|$$

is $c_1 = A_1, \cdots, c_N = A_N$, where each $A_n$ is the Fourier coefficient

$$A_n = \frac{\langle f, X_n \rangle}{\| X_n \|^2}.$$

주어진 $L^2$ 함수 $f$를 orthogonal function들로 잘 approximation하려고 할 때 가장 최적의 근사는 각 계수들을 푸리에 계수로 주는 방법임을 말해준다. 

### Proof
Denote the error by 

$$E_N := \left \| f - \sum_{n=1}^N c_nX_n \right \|^2$$

Then we have 

$$\begin{align*}
E_N &= \left \| f - \sum_{n=1}^N c_nX_n \right \|^2 \\
&= \int_a^b \left \vert f(x) - \sum_{n=1}^N c_nX_n(x) \right \vert^2 \, dx \\
&= \int_a^b \vert f(x) \vert^2 \, dx - 2 \sum_{n=1}^N c_n \int_a^b f(x) X_n(x) \, dx + \sum_{n=1}^N \sum_{m=1}^N c_nc_m \int_a^b X_n(x)X_m(x) \, dx \\
&= \| f \|^2 - 2 \sum_{n=1}^N c_n \langle f, X_n \rangle + \sum_{n=1}^N c_n^2 \| X_n \|^2 \\
&= \sum_{n=1}^N \| X_n \|^2 \left[c_n - \frac{\langle f, X_n \rangle}{\| X_n \|^2} \right]^2 + \| f \|^2 - \sum_{n=1}^N \frac{\langle f, X_n \rangle^2}{\| X_n \|^2}.
\end{align*}$$


Since the first term 

$$\sum_{n=1}^N \| X_n \|^2 \left[c_n - \frac{\langle f, X_n \rangle}{\| X_n \|^2} \right]^2$$

is alway nonnegative, $E_N$ is smallest when 

$$c_n = \frac{\langle f, X_n \rangle}{\| X_n \|^2} = A_n$$

which is the Fourier coefficient. $\blacksquare$

## Bessel's Inequality
Let $\\{ X_n \\}$ be any orthogonal set of functions, and let $\| f \| < \infty.$ Then

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n^2 \| X_n \|^2 \le \| f \|^2.$$

### Proof
From the least-square approximation of $f$, we get 

$$E_N = \| f \|^2 - \sum_{n=1}^N A_n^2 \| X_n \|^2 \ge 0,$$

so that 

$$\sum_{n=1}^N A_n^2 \| X_n \|^2 \le \| f \|^2.$$

Note that the sequence of the partial sums 

$$\sum_{n=1}^N A_n^2 \| X_n \|^2$$

is bounded and increasing. By the monotone convergence theorem, it converges and 

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n^2 \| X_n \|^2 \le \| f \|^2. \quad \blacksquare$$

## Theorem 2 (Parseval's Equality)
The Fourier series of $f$ converges to $f$ in $L^2$-sense $\iff$ The Bessel's inequality becomes the equality. That is, 

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n^2 \| X_n \|^2 = \| f \|^2.$$

This equality is called the ***Parseval's equality.***

### Proof
The statement that the Fourier series of $f$ converges to $f$ in $L^2$-sense is equivalent to the error goes to zero, that is, 

$$\lim_{N \to \infty} E_N = 0.$$

Thus, we have

$$\begin{align*}
0 &= \lim_{N \to \infty} E_N \\
&= \lim_{N \to \infty} \left[ \| f \|^2 - \sum_{n=1}^N A_n^2 \| X_n \|^2 \right],
\end{align*}$$

which means that 

$$ \| f \|^2 = \sum_{n=1}^N A_n^2 \| X_n \|^2. \quad \blacksquare$$

---
## Corollary
If $\| f \| < \infty$, then the Parseval's equality is true.

### Proof
If $\| f \| < \infty$, then the Fourier series converges in $L^2$-sence to $f(x)$. By the above theorem, the Parseval's equality is true. $\blacksquare$

## Basel Problem
Calculate 

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2}.$$

### Solution
Note that the Fourier sine series of $f(x) = 1$ on $(0, \pi)$ 

$$\sum_{n: \text{odd}} \frac{4}{n\pi} \sin nx.$$

converges pointwise to $f(x)$ on $(0, \pi).$ 

Since $\| f \| < \infty$, the Parseval's equality holds:

$$\begin{align*}
\pi &= \int_0^\pi 1^2 \, dx \\
&= \| f \|^2 \\
&= \sum_{n: \text{odd}} \left( \frac{4}{n\pi} \right)^2 \| \sin nx \|^2 \\
&= \sum_{n: \text{odd}} \frac{16}{n^2 \pi^2} \int_0^\pi \sin^2 nx \, dx \\
&= \sum_{n: \text{odd}} \frac{16}{n^2 \pi^2} \frac{\pi}{2} \\
&= \sum_{n: \text{odd}} \frac{8}{n^2 \pi},
\end{align*}$$

so we obtain 

$$\sum_{n: \text{odd}} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{8}.$$

Note that the given series is convergent by $p$-series test. Denote 

$$S := \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2}.$$

Note that 

$$\begin{align*}
\sum_{n: \text{even}} \frac{1}{n^2} &= \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{(2n)^2} \\
&= \frac{1}{4} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \\
&= \frac{S}{4}.
\end{align*}$$

Then we have 

$$\begin{align*}
S &= \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \\
&= \sum_{n: \text{odd}} \frac{1}{n^2} + \sum_{n: \text{even}} \frac{1}{n^2} \\
&= \frac{\pi^2}{8} + \frac{S}{4},
\end{align*}$$

so we get 

$$S = \frac{\pi^2}{6}. \quad \blacksquare$$
