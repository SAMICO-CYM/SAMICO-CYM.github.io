## Theorem 1
Let $\\{ X_\alpha \\} _ {\alpha \in J}$ be an indexed family of topological spaces, and let $\displaystyle X := \prod _ {\alpha \in J} X_\alpha$. 

**(i)** Let 

$$\mathcal{B} = \left\{ \prod _ {\alpha \in J} U_\alpha \,\, \Bigg \vert \,\, U_\alpha \text{ is open in } X_\alpha.  \right\}.$$

Then $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis on $X$. The topology generated by $\mathcal{B}$ is called the ***box topology*** on $X$.

**(ii)** Let $\pi _ {\beta}: X \to X_{\beta}$ be the projection map for each $\beta \in J$. Let 

$$\mathcal{S} = \left\{ \pi^{-1} _ {\beta}(U_\beta) \,\, \Bigg \vert \,\, U_\beta \text{ is open in } X_\beta, \beta \in J \right\}.$$

Then $\mathcal{S}$ is a subbasis on $X$. The topology generated by $\mathcal{S}$ is called the ***product topology*** on $X$, and $X$ with this topology is called a ***product space***.

### Proof
**(i)** Let $x \in X$. Then $x = \\{ x_\alpha \\} _ {\alpha \in J}$. Since $x_\alpha \in X_\alpha$ for each $\alpha \in J$, $x \in X$. Since $X \in \mathcal{B}$, the fisrt condition is satisfied.

Suppose that $x \in B_1 \cap B_2$ for some $B_1, B_2 \in \mathcal{B}$. Then we have $\displaystyle B_1 = \prod _ {\alpha \in J} U_\alpha$ and $\displaystyle B_2 = \prod _ {\alpha \in J} V_\alpha$ where $U_\alpha$ and $V_\alpha$ are open in $X_\alpha$ for each $\alpha \in J$. 

Since $x \in B_1 \cap B_2$, we have that $x_\alpha \in U_\alpha \cap V_\alpha$ for each $\alpha \in J$. Since $U_\alpha$ and $V_\alpha$ are open in $X_\alpha$, $U_\alpha \cap V_\alpha$ is open in $X_\alpha$. Let $\displaystyle B_3 = \prod _ {\alpha \in J} (U_\alpha \cap V_\alpha)$. Then $B_3 \in \mathcal{B}$ and $x \in B_3 \subseteq B_1 \cap B_2$. Thus, $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis on $X$.

**(ii)** Clearly, we have $\displaystyle X \supset \bigcup \mathcal{S}.$ Since $\pi^{-1} _ {\beta}(X_\beta) = X$ for all $\beta \in J$, it follows that $\displaystyle X \subset \bigcup \mathcal{S}.$ Thus, we have $\displaystyle X = \bigcup \mathcal{S}$, which means that $\mathcal{S}$ is a subbasis on $X$. $\blacksquare$

## Remark
Note that $\displaystyle \pi^{-1} _ {\beta}(U_\beta) = \prod _ {\alpha \in J} U_\alpha$, where 

$$U_\alpha = \begin{cases} 
U_\beta, & \text{if } \alpha = \beta \\ 
X_\alpha, & \text{if } \alpha \neq \beta. 
\end{cases}$$

That is, when $J = \mathbb{N}$, $\pi^{-1} _ {\beta}(U_\beta) = X_1 \times \cdots \times X _ {\beta - 1} \times U_\beta \times X _ {\beta + 1} \times \cdots.$

Hence, the basis $\mathcal{B}'$ generated by $\mathcal{S}$ is 

$$\mathcal{B}' = \left\{ \prod _ {\alpha \in J} U_\alpha \,\, \Bigg \vert \,\, U_\alpha = \begin{cases} 
U_\beta, & \text{if } \alpha = \beta \in I \\ 
X_\alpha, & \text{otherwise} 
\end{cases}, U_\beta \text{ is open in } X_\beta, I \subset J, \vert I \vert < \infty. \right\}$$

## Theorem 2
Let $\{ X_\alpha \} _ {\alpha \in J}$ be an indexed family of topological spaces, and let $\displaystyle X := \prod _ {\alpha \in J} X_\alpha$. Let $\mathcal{B}$ and $\mathcal{B}'$ be the bases, described above, for the box topology and the product topology on $X$, respectively. 

(i) If $\vert J \vert < \infty$, then $\mathcal{B} = \mathcal{B}'$.

(ii) If $\vert J \vert = \infty$, then $\mathcal{B}' \subset \mathcal{B}$, that is, the box topology is finer than the product topology on $X$.
