## Definition
**(i)** A graph is called a ***tree*** if it is connected and has no cycle.

**(ii)** A vertex of degree one is called a ***leaf***.

---
## Theorem 1
For a graph $T$, TFAE.

**(i)** $T$ is a tree.

**(ii)** For any vertices $u, v \in V(T)$, there is a unique $uv$-path in $T$.

**(iii)** $T$ is connected, but is disconnected by the removal of any edge.

**(iv)** $T$ has no cylce, but has a cycle on the addition of any edge.

**(v)** $\vert V(T) \vert = \vert E(T) \vert + 1$.

---
## Theorem 2
For a finite tree $T$, it has a leaf $\ell$. Furthermore, $T - \ell$ is also a tree.

### Proof
Let $P = v_1 \cdots v_r$ be a longest path in $T$. If $v_r$ is a leaf, then we are done. If $v_r$ is not a leaf, then it has an edge diffrent from $\\{ v_{r-1}, v_r \\}$. This must not be to a vertex in $P$, but to some other vertex walking a longer path than $P$. $\bigotimes$ Thus, $T$ has a leaf. 

Let $T$ be a tree with a leaf $\ell$. If $V(T) = n$ and $E(T) = m$, then we have $n = m - 1$ by Theoerm 1 (v). If we remove a left $\ell$ from $T$, then a graph $T - \ell$ is a graph with $n-1$ vertices and $m-1$ edges. Since $(n-1) = (m-1) - 1$, we conclude that $T - \ell$ is also a tree. $\blacksquare$
